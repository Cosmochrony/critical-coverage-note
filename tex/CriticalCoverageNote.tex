  \begin{abstract}
    The auto-calibrated saturation depth $\none(q)$ of the breadth-first exploration of
    $\Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ was the central open asymptotic variable of O25--O28~\cite{Beau2026a29,Beau2026a32}.
    This note makes two points.
    First, the depth problem is not a vector-space filling problem: at the window edge the cumulative
    Weil--fingerprint rank is far from full, so $\none$ is the depth at which the creation of new
    directions per explored vertex drops below a fixed threshold --- a stop of marginal novelty.
    Second, and consequently, the entire asymptotic content of $\none$ collapses onto a single
    dimensionless observable, the \emph{critical coverage}
    \[
      \xone(q) = \frac{\Bball_{\,n=\none}}{q^2},
      \qquad\text{through the exact identity}\qquad
      \none(q) = \left(\frac{\xone(q)}{\Cheis}\right)^{1/4} \sqrt{q},
    \]
    where $\Bball \sim \Cheis\,n^4$ (homogeneous dimension $D=4$, $\Cheis \approx 0.427$) is the
    Heisenberg ball growth.
    The reduction rests on two robust facts --- $\Bball \sim \Cheis\,n^4$ and the diffusive spectral gap
    $\lambda_2 \approx 4\pi^2/q^2$ --- and one scaling ansatz.
    Empirically $\xone$ falls from $0.61$ at $q=101$ to $0.15$ at $q=601$, which excludes the
    constant-coverage (clean $\sqrt{q}$) scenario and supports the model-free conclusion
    $\none(q)/q \to 0$.
    We isolate the governing tension $\Phi'(\xone) = \epssat\,q$ and state the analytical programme:
    derive the asymptotic law of $\xone(q)$ from a shellwise Weil--BFS novelty estimate controlled by
    $\lambda_2$, of which the depth law $\none(q)$ is then a corollary.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{The saturation-depth problem}
    \label{sec:problem}

    Let $q$ be an odd prime, $G_q = \Heis_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, and $\Cay(G_q, S_q)$ its Cayley
    graph with symmetric generating set $S_q = \{X^{\pm 1}, Y^{\pm 1}\}$.
    Breadth-first search from the identity produces spheres $S_n$ and balls $B_n = \bigcup_{m \le n} S_m$.
    Each shell carries Weil $k=3$ fingerprint vectors in $\mathbb{C}^q$; their cumulative
    Gram--Schmidt rank $r(n) = \sum_{m \le n}\Delta r(m)$ is monotone and bounded by $q$, and the
    capacity-novelty density is
    \begin{equation}
      \sigma(n) = \frac{\Delta r(n)}{\lvert S_n\rvert}.
      \label{eq:sigma}
    \end{equation}
    The auto-calibrated fitting window of O12~\cite{Beau2026a16} has upper edge
    \begin{equation}
      \none(q) = \text{last } n \text{ with } \bar{\sigma}(n) > \epssat,
      \qquad \epssat = 10^{-3},
      \label{eq:n1def}
    \end{equation}
    where $\bar\sigma$ is the mean over the conjugate-pair probe blocks of~\eqref{eq:sigma}.

    \begin{remark}[The depth is a marginal-novelty stop, not a filling time]
      \label{rem:marginal}
      At the window edge the cumulative rank is far from full: $r(\none) \approx 137/307$ at $q=307$ and
      $183/601$ at $q=601$.
      So $\none$ is \emph{not} the depth at which the fingerprints span $\mathbb{C}^q$.
      By~\eqref{eq:n1def} it is the depth at which the marginal rank gained per explored vertex,
      $\sigma(n)$, first falls below the fixed threshold $\epssat$.
      This reformulation is model-free and survives independently of the eventual asymptotic law; it is
      also the form that transfers to the per-generation depths $n_g$, which are the same kind of object
      (a marginal-novelty stop read off the relaxation trajectory, not a rank count).
    \end{remark}

    O25--O28~\cite{Beau2026a29,Beau2026a32} left the asymptotic law of $\none(q)$ as the central open
    direction.
    O28 settled its gross shape model-free: the linear hypothesis $\none \sim \hat\alpha\,q$ calibrated
    on $q \le 211$ fails out of sample (it overpredicts $\none$ by up to $+88\%$ at $q=601$), and
    $\none(q)/q$ decreases monotonically to $0.032$, so $\none(q)/q \to 0$.
    The asymptotic law itself remained open.
    The present note reduces that open law to a single observable.

  \section{The critical coverage and the reduction}
    \label{sec:reduction}

    Two ingredients of the depth law are already understood.

    \begin{proposition}[Volume growth, $D=4$]
      \label{prop:growth}
      On $\Cay(G_q, S_q)$ the homogeneous dimension is $D = 4$ (Bass--Guivarc'h; O9~\cite{Beau2026a13}),
      so for $n \ll q$,
      \begin{equation}
        \Bball = \Cheis\,n^4\,(1 + o(1)),
        \qquad \Cheis \approx 0.427,
        \label{eq:growth}
      \end{equation}
      with shell ratio $\lvert S_n\rvert / n^3 \to 4\Cheis \approx 1.71$.
      The fitted exponent is $p = 4.003$.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proposition}[Diffusive spectral gap]
      \label{prop:gap}
      The algebraic connectivity of $\Cay(G_q, S_q)$ satisfies
      \begin{equation}
        \lambda_2(L_{G_q}) \approx \frac{4\pi^2}{q^2},
        \label{eq:gap}
      \end{equation}
      the gap of the horizontal abelianisation torus $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2$
      (numerically $\lambda_2\,q^2 \to \approx 39 \to 4\pi^2 = 39.48$ over $q \in \{5,\dots,19\}$).
      The walk relaxes diffusively, and its horizontal phase space has $q^2$ vertices.
    \end{proposition}

    These fix the natural coverage variable: a diffusive walk with gap $q^{-2}$ on a space of horizontal
    extent $q^2$ is organised by the reduced volume $x = \Bball/q^2$.
    Define the \emph{critical coverage} at the window edge:

    \begin{definition}[Critical coverage]
      \label{def:x1}
      \begin{equation}
        \xone(q) = \frac{\Bball_{\,n=\none}}{q^2}
        = \frac{\Cheis\,\none(q)^4}{q^2}\,(1 + o(1)).
        \label{eq:x1}
      \end{equation}
    \end{definition}

    Inverting~\eqref{eq:growth} at the crossing volume $\Bball_{\,n=\none} = \xone(q)\,q^2$ gives the
    organising identity of this note.

    \begin{proposition}[Reduction of the depth problem to the coverage problem]
      \label{prop:reduction}
      Exactly (to leading order in~\eqref{eq:growth}),
      \begin{equation}
        \none(q) = \left(\frac{\xone(q)}{\Cheis}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{q}.
        \label{eq:reduction}
      \end{equation}
      Hence the asymptotic law of $\none(q)$ \emph{is} the asymptotic law of $\xone(q)$: a constant
      $\xone$ gives $\none = \Theta(\sqrt q)$; a decaying $\xone$ gives a sub-$\sqrt q$ law.
      In every case $\xone \le O(1)$ keeps $\none = O(\sqrt q)$, so $\none/q \to 0$.
    \end{proposition}

    The protagonist of the saturation-depth problem is therefore $\xone(q)$, not $\none(q)$.

    Two readings of the same table.

    The striking empirical fact is this sustained fall of the coverage: the vertex coverage at which the
    crossing $\sigma(\none) = \epssat$ occurs keeps moving to ever-lower values as $q$ grows.

  \section{Exclusion of the constant-coverage scenario}
    \label{sec:exclusion}

    By Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} the candidate depth laws are candidate coverage laws:
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
      \item $\xone \to \mathrm{const}$ $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ clean $\Theta(\sqrt q)$;
      \item $\xone \propto (\ln q)^{-4}$ $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ $\none \approx \kappa\sqrt q/\ln q$;
      \item $\xone \propto q^{-2/3}$ $\;\Longrightarrow\;$ $\none \propto q^{1/3}$.
    \end{itemize}

    The point $q = 601$ excludes the constant-coverage scenario.
    The apparent plateau $\xone = 0.222\,(401) \to 0.220\,(503)$ suggested convergence, which would have
    revived a clean asymptotic $\sqrt q$ with $\kappa \approx 0.85$; but the constant-$\xone$ reading
    predicts $\none(601) = (0.22/\Cheis)^{1/4}\sqrt{601} \approx 20.8$ (i.e.\ $21$), whereas the
    measurement gives $19$.
    The stall was an integer-rounding artefact ($\none$ held the value $19$ across $q = 503$ and the
    $q = 601$ neighbourhood), not a limit: $\xone$ resumed its decrease to $0.154$.

    The two surviving readings, $\none \approx \kappa\sqrt q/\ln q$ and $\none \propto q^{1/3}$, are both
    sub-$\sqrt q$ and remain numerically indistinguishable on the present data (each predicts
    $\none(601) = 20$ against the measured $19$; they differ by less than one unit in $\none$ out to
    $q \sim 10^3$).
    The effective exponent observed over $101 \le q \le 601$ is $\approx 0.30\text{--}0.33$, below
    $\tfrac{1}{2}$, but this is a range observation, not an established asymptotic value: the $\none$ are
    small integers and the $503 \to 601$ step is $19 \to 19$, which dominates any power fit.
    What is established for $\none$ is therefore: sub-linear, incompatible with $\Theta(q)$, and --- over
    the observed range --- below $\Theta(\sqrt q)$; the precise asymptotic law is the open law of $\xone(q)$.

  \section{The governing tension}
    \label{sec:tension}

    The reduction~\eqref{eq:reduction} is exact; what is \emph{not} yet proved is the law of $\xone$.
    The structural route to it is the coverage-scaling ansatz suggested by
    Propositions~\ref{prop:growth}--\ref{prop:gap}:
    \begin{equation}
      r_q(n) \approx q\,\Phi\!\left(\frac{\Bball}{q^2}\right),
      \qquad \Phi \text{ smooth},\; \Phi(0)=0,\; \Phi \text{ increasing}.
      \tag{$\star$}
      \label{eq:star}
    \end{equation}
    This is a scaling collapse: the $q$-dependence of the whole rank trajectory is assumed to enter only
    through the reduced coverage $x = \Bball/q^2$.
    Granting~\eqref{eq:star}, and using $\sigma(n) = \Delta r/\lvert S_n\rvert \approx \mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}\Bball$
    (the marginal density of Remark~\ref{rem:marginal}), one gets $\sigma(n) = \Phi'(x)/q$, so the window
    criterion $\sigma(\none) = \epssat$ reads
    \begin{equation}
      \Phi'(\xone) = \epssat\,q.
      \label{eq:tension}
    \end{equation}
    The right-hand side \emph{grows with $q$} while $\epssat = 10^{-3}$ is fixed.
    Equation~\eqref{eq:tension} is the single mathematical front of the problem: a fixed threshold against
    the explicit $1/q$ in $\sigma$.
    It forces the crossing coverage $\xone$ into the steep part of $\Phi$ as $q$ grows, hence the observed
    decay of $\xone$; whether that decay is logarithmic or a residual power is exactly the unresolved
    behaviour of $\xone(q)$, and explaining it --- rather than fitting an exponent --- is the task.

  \section{Analytical programme for the law of \texorpdfstring{$\xone(q)$}{x1(q)}}
    \label{sec:programme}

    The target is a structural law for $\xone(q)$, from which $\none(q)$ follows by~\eqref{eq:reduction}.
    The proposed route has three steps.
    \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
      \item \textbf{Shellwise novelty estimate.}
        Bound the per-shell rank increment $\Delta r(n)$ of the Weil $k=3$ fingerprints by the mixing of
        the BFS distribution at depth $n$, controlled by the diffusive gap $\lambda_2(L_{G_q})$
        of~\eqref{eq:gap}.
        This is the missing derivation of the ansatz~\eqref{eq:star}: it should produce $\Phi$, not assume
        it.
      \item \textbf{Crossing analysis.}
        Solve~\eqref{eq:tension} with the derived $\Phi$ to obtain $\xone(q)$, and hence the asymptotic
        law and any logarithmic or power correction.
      \item \textbf{Falsifiable prediction.}
        Extend the campaign to $q \in \{809, 1009, 1201\}$ to test the predicted $\xone(q)$ directly
        (the coverage, not a fitted exponent, is the observable).
    \end{enumerate}

    The same machinery is the bridge to the per-generation depths $n_g$: by Remark~\ref{rem:marginal} they
    are marginal-novelty stops governed by the same relaxation $\lambda_2$, so a law for $\xone$ is the
    template for the corresponding per-generation coverages.

  \section{Status}
    \label{sec:status}

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
      \item \emph{Proved / robust:} $D = 4$ growth, $\Bball \sim \Cheis n^4$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:growth});
        $\lambda_2 = \Theta(1/q^2)$, $\approx 4\pi^2/q^2$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:gap}); and the exact reduction
        identity~\eqref{eq:reduction} (Prop.~\ref{prop:reduction}).
      \item \emph{Established empirically / model-free (range-limited):} $\none(q)/q \to 0$ (the linear
        law is excluded out of sample, O28); $\xone(q)$ decreases ($0.61 \to 0.15$ over
        $q = 101\text{--}601$); the constant-coverage scenario is excluded; the observed effective
        exponent of $\none$ is $\approx 0.30\text{--}0.33$.
      \item \emph{Open:} the asymptotic law of $\xone(q)$ (logarithmic vs.\ power), the ansatz~\eqref{eq:star}
        and its $\Phi$, and the resolution of the tension~\eqref{eq:tension}.
    \end{itemize}
